# The Lore of the Shattered Covenant

---

## 1. The Covenant

Before time had meaning, reality was singular and whole — one world, one existence, governed by the **Covenant**. The Covenant was not written, not spoken, not enforced by any hand. It simply *was* — the sacred grammar of existence, the invisible architecture that held all forces in balance. Think of it as the oath that held the axis of the world straight: primal, absolute, and as natural as gravity.

The Covenant bound five great forces into coexistence. These forces were not gods. They had no personalities, no desires, no names. They were aspects of existence itself — as fundamental and impersonal as pressure, as old as the concept of "something rather than nothing." They were:

- **Dominion** — the principle of authority, hierarchy, and order
- **Entropy** — the principle of change, decay, and transformation
- **Communion** — the principle of connection, unity, and empathy
- **Wrath** — the principle of conflict, passion, and violent creation
- **Memory** — the principle of preservation, knowledge, and continuity

Under the Covenant, these forces coexisted in a single reality. Where Dominion built structures, Entropy ensured they would eventually fall. Where Wrath tore things apart, Communion wove new relationships from the wreckage. Where Memory preserved the past, Entropy ensured it would not petrify the present. Each force checked and complemented the others. The world was not peaceful — it was *balanced*.

---

## 2. The Sundering

Then one of the forces broke the Covenant.

The reason is lost. Perhaps it was erased from Memory. Perhaps it was an act of ambition, or inevitability, or mercy, or madness. What is known is that the Covenant — primordial and absolute as it was — was not infrangible. It could be broken. And it was.

The moment the Covenant shattered, balance ceased. The five forces, no longer bound to coexist, could not tolerate each other's proximity. Reality fractured. Each force retreated into its own domain, crystallising a realm around itself — a pocket of existence where it reigned supreme and unchecked. Where once there was one world with all forces in tension, now there were five **shards**, drifting through the void like fragments of a shattered vessel.

But the ghost of the Covenant lingers. The shards are still drawn toward each other — gravitationally, inevitably — as if reality remembers what it was and is trying, blindly, to become whole again. This is the source of the realms' drift. This is why they will one day collide and merge. This is **the Convergence**.

---

## 3. The Ordained

### What They Are

When the Covenant existed, it required instruments — hands through which its balance could be maintained. These instruments were **the Ordained**: entities consecrated by the Covenant itself, imbued with a portion of its authority. They were not rulers. They were not servants. They were **the Covenant's will made manifest** — sacred agents who intervened when a force strayed from balance, who corrected imbalances, who maintained the architecture of reality.

The Ordained were feared and revered by the forces. They were the only things in existence that held authority *over* the fundamental aspects of reality. This was not power they earned — it was power they were created to carry.

### The Fall

When the Covenant shattered, the Ordained did not die. But they were unmade in every way that mattered. The authority they carried — the Covenant's authority — drained from them like blood from an opened vein. Their purpose evaporated. The forces they once corrected fled into separate realms and no longer answered to them. The inhabitants of the new realms had no memory of them. The Ordained became **remnants** — beings that still existed but no longer had reason to.

### The Hunger

But in the ruin of their purpose, the Ordained discovered something new: **freedom**. For the first time in their existence, they were not instruments. They had no mandate. They could *want*. And what they want, with the cold clarity of beings who once held the axis of reality in their hands, is this: **to never serve again**.

Each of the Ordained now seeks to accumulate enough power that when the Convergence inevitably reassembles reality, **they will be the one defining its new order** — not serving it, not maintaining it, but *ruling* it. Every dominion they build is a piece of that future order. Every religion they found is a lie designed to funnel souls into their grasp. Every rival Ordained is a threat to their freedom.

They are selfish because they tasted what it means to exist without purpose, and they will destroy anything that tries to give them one.

### Visibility

The Ordained are **visible** to the inhabitants of the realms — but clearly not of their world. They manifest as figures surrounded by a faint distortion: a shimmer in the light, a pressure in the air, a silence that follows them. They are unsettling and obviously powerful. They are not recorded in any myth, legend, or scripture — the Sundering erased the old world entirely. To the inhabitants, an Ordained is an **unprecedented phenomenon** — a being of obvious, alien authority with no explanation.

When an Ordained arrives at a dominion, it is an event. People kneel, or flee, or stare. They do not understand what they are seeing. But they recognise power when it stands before them.

## 4. The Five Realms

Each realm is a shard of the old reality, crystallised around a single force. Without the other forces to check it, each has grown **extreme** — a domain where one principle dominates to the point of distortion. The realms are not evil or good. They are *unbalanced*.

### 4.1 The Realm of Dominion — The Eternal Hierarchy

A realm of absolute order, rigid caste systems, and monolithic authority. Massive citadels of grey stone stretch endlessly upward. Every person has a rank, a role, a place — and none may change it. The force of Dominion, unchecked, has created a world where **structure has replaced life**. Obedience is not taught here; it is woven into the fabric of reality. A commoner cannot disobey a lord any more than water can flow uphill.

**Landscape:** Geometric, precise. Cities arranged in concentric rings of authority. Roads straight as drawn lines. Uniform architecture. Everything in its place.

**Inhabitants:** Obedient, rigid, deeply hierarchical. Strong rulers. Loyal soldiers. Passive commoners. They accept authority as natural law.

**Energy — Authority:** Power flows downward through hierarchy. Rites performed here involve command, control, and unbreakable order. Dominions in this realm are exceptionally stable — subjects don't rebel, decay is slow, subversion is difficult. But expansion is hard: the realm resists change, and new dominions must be established through legitimate channels of authority, not conquest.

**Strategic profile:** The safest realm to hold. The hardest to break into. Religions here are rigid and spread slowly but are nearly impossible to dislodge once rooted. Low soul turnover (people live long, orderly lives and die predictably). A realm for players who build deep, not wide.

### 4.2 The Realm of Entropy — The Ever-Dying Land

A realm of constant transformation. Great civilisations bloom and crumble within generations. Forests rise as you watch and rot by nightfall. The ground itself shifts — mountains erode into valleys, rivers change course, coastlines advance and recede. The force of Entropy, unchecked, has created a world where **nothing persists**. Death here is not an ending but a transition — everything is becoming something else, always.

**Landscape:** Lush, decaying, beautiful and terrible simultaneously. Ruins covered in new growth. Cities built on the bones of older cities. Everything is impermanent and visibly so.

**Inhabitants:** Adaptable, fatalistic, resourceful. They build quickly, knowing it will not last. Life is intense and short. Death is common and unremarked upon.

**Energy — Transformation:** Everything changes. Rites performed here accelerate change — topple rulers, dissolve borders, mutate religions, transform armies into something new. Dominions in this realm are easy to establish but impossible to maintain without constant attention. Religions spread fast but mutate and drift.

**Strategic profile:** High risk, high reward. The best realm for rapid soul generation (high mortality, fast religious turnover). The worst for stable holdings. A realm for players who move fast and adapt faster.

### 4.3 The Realm of Communion — The Interwoven

A realm where boundaries blur. Cities bleed into each other across landscapes that have no clear borders. Thoughts pass between minds like wind. Identity is fluid — a person might wake up with memories that aren't theirs, beliefs they didn't choose, loyalties they can't explain. The force of Communion, unchecked, has dissolved **individuality** into collective consciousness.

**Landscape:** Organic, flowing, interconnected. Networks of roads, rivers, and root systems that link everything to everything. No walls. No gates. No clear edges.

**Inhabitants:** Empathic, communal, porous. They share beliefs, feelings, and sometimes consciousness itself. Strong collective cultures. Weak individual identity. They adopt new ideas easily but are just as easily influenced by neighbouring ideas.

**Energy — Connection:** Creates bonds between things — people, places, concepts. Rites performed here link dominions across distances, spread religion with extraordinary speed, merge populations, or establish communication. But the connections go both ways — influence flows back toward you from your subjects, and secrets are nearly impossible to keep within this realm.

**Strategic profile:** The best realm for religion — beliefs spread rapidly through the web of connections. The most dangerous realm for hidden plans — information leaks. Dominions here are easy to influence but hard to control exclusively, since neighbouring influence bleeds through. A realm for players who build wide religious networks and don't mind transparency.

### 4.4 The Realm of Wrath — The Crucible

A realm of perpetual conflict and fierce independence. Not a hellscape — Wrath here is the fire that forges, the storm that clears, the war that births nations. Every settlement is a fortress. Every border is contested. The force of Wrath, unchecked, has created a world where **strength is the only truth** and peace is a pause between battles.

**Landscape:** Harsh, dramatic, beautiful in a violent way. Volcanic forges, storm-wracked plateaus, shattered battlefields that have become sacred ground. Cities are built like weapons.

**Inhabitants:** Warlike, passionate, fiercely independent. They respect strength and despise weakness. They do not follow doctrine — they follow leaders who prove themselves in combat. Loyalty is won through blood, not words.

**Energy — Force:** Raw, destructive, creative. Rites here involve combat, sacrifice, and the application of overwhelming power. Armies recruited here are the strongest in the game. But religions founded on Wrath struggle — the inhabitants are too independent to follow doctrine quietly. Souls are won in battle, not in peaceful death.

**Strategic profile:** The military realm. Best for building powerful armies and aggressive expansion. Poor for religion (inhabitants don't follow beliefs, they follow strength). High soul generation through warfare but routing is difficult. A realm for players who conquer.

### 4.5 The Realm of Memory — The Archive

A realm where nothing is forgotten. Every event is preserved in the fabric of reality. Every word echoes. Every deed leaves a permanent mark. The force of Memory, unchecked, has created a world **crushed under the weight of its own past**. Ancient structures are not ruins — they are still alive, still functioning, as perfectly preserved as the moment they were built. The inhabitants live among ghosts — not of the dead, but of every moment that ever was, replaying forever.

**Landscape:** Layered, dense, overwhelming. Cities built on top of cities, each layer visible through the next. Libraries that contain not books but preserved experiences. Monuments to every event ever recorded.

**Inhabitants:** Scholarly, cautious, burdened. They know too much. Every decision is weighed against the full record of history. They are wise but slow to act, thoughtful but paralysed by awareness. New ideas struggle to take root because every old idea is still actively present.

**Energy — Preservation:** Things endure here beyond their natural span. Rites performed here freeze a dominion's state, recall old alliances, access fragments of Covenant-era knowledge, and prevent decay entirely. But the weight of Memory makes change nearly impossible — new religions struggle against the presence of every religion that has ever existed in that place.

**Strategic profile:** The most defensible realm. Everything persists, including your control. But the hardest to penetrate as an outsider (the realm remembers who belongs). Rich in ancient knowledge — relics and rites of the old Covenant are most accessible here. A realm for players who build indestructible positions and play the long game.

## 5. Souls

### What Souls Are

When the Covenant existed, every living being was born under its protection — bound by its laws, sustained by its balance. That binding left an **imprint** on every living thing: a thread connecting them to the structure of reality. This imprint is what is called a soul. It is not consciousness, not personality, not spirit. It is the **mark of the old order** — proof that this being existed within the Covenant's framework.

### Why Souls Matter

When a being dies, the imprint (soul) is released. Under the Covenant, it would have returned to the structure, reinforcing the whole — a natural cycle. Now, with the Covenant shattered, loose souls drift without destination. The Ordained can **claim them**. Each soul absorbed is a fragment of the old order's authority, making the Ordained more real, more powerful, more capable of imposing their will.

### Souls as Currency

To perform a rite — to bend reality using fragments of the old Covenant's power — requires fuel. That fuel is souls. Spending a soul is burning a piece of the old order to temporarily recreate its effects. This creates the game's core tension: **hoard souls to win (most souls at the Convergence) or spend them to act (rites, relics, rituals).**

### Soul Routing

When an inhabitant dies, their soul follows the path laid by their beliefs — the religion they followed in life. The religion's **belief tags** determine which Ordained the soul is drawn to. A religion founded by Player A, with belief tags aligned to Player A's collection, will route souls to Player A upon followers' deaths.

Religions are the primary mechanism for soul routing, but not the only one. Souls can also be gained through: performing rites of harvesting, pillaging sacred sites, winning battles (the souls of fallen warriors), and completing certain missions.

---

## 6. Religions

### What Religions Are

Religions are **sacred oaths** — sets of beliefs that an Ordained teaches to mortal inhabitants. These beliefs function as binding vows, pale echoes of the Covenant. When a believer dies, their soul follows the path their vows describe — drawn to the Ordained who taught them those beliefs.

Every religion is a lie. The Ordained do not believe in the doctrines they preach. They construct religions as **soul-routing mechanisms**, engineered for mechanical advantage. The inhabitants, of course, do not know this. To them, their faith is real.

### How Religions Differ from Dominions

Dominions are about **political control** — ruling through a leader, backed by presence and authority. Religions are about **spiritual influence** — shaping beliefs, backed by doctrine and cultural spread. A player can have a dominion without a religion (secular rule), a religion without a dominion (underground faith), or both in the same place (theocratic control).

### Religions and the Realms

Each realm's energy affects how religions behave:
- **Dominion:** Religions are rigid and state-enforced. Slow to spread, impossible to uproot.
- **Entropy:** Religions spread fast but mutate and drift. Unstable but prolific.
- **Communion:** Religions spread like wildfire through interconnection. But they also absorb competing beliefs, becoming hybrid.
- **Wrath:** Religions of action and strength. Spread through conquest, not conversion. Fierce but undisciplined.
- **Memory:** Ancient religions endure forever. New religions cannot displace old ones. Layered faiths competing for the same followers.

## 7. All Realm Merge Combinations

> **Design principle:** Each merge is MORE beneficial than any of its components alone. When realms overlap, their combined energies create new capabilities that neither realm offered individually. As the game progresses toward the Convergence, the world becomes richer and more powerful, not simpler.

### 7.4 The Full Merge — The Convergence

**All Five: Dominion + Entropy + Communion + Wrath + Memory**

**The Restored World**

When all five forces occupy the same space, reality is — for the first time since the Sundering — **whole**. But it is not the same as what came before. The forces are no longer balanced by the Covenant. They are balanced by **pressure** — five uncheck forces crammed into one space, each pushing against all the others.

The Convergence zone is the most complex and powerful area in the game:
- All rites from all individual realms and merges are available
- Religions spread through connection, enforced by authority, fuelled by passion, preserved by memory, and constantly adapting through entropy
- Armies are powerful, disciplined, zealous, ever-remembered, and constantly evolving
- Information flows freely (Communion + Memory) but the world changes constantly (Entropy)
- Everything that was built throughout the game remains (Memory) but nothing is safe from change (Entropy)

**New capability — the Convergence Rites:** These are the most powerful rites in the game, available only here:
- **Rite of the New Covenant:** The instant-win objective. Requires massive soul expenditure, control of key positions, and specific relics. If completed, the performing Ordained becomes the new Covenant — the new law of reality — and wins immediately.
- **Rite of Supremacy:** Declare yourself the dominant authority. All dominions in the Convergence zone that are not actively attended by their owner are contested. A power grab for the endgame.
- **Rite of Reckoning:** Force a scoring check. All religions settle their soul debts immediately. Can be used to lock in a lead or trigger chaos.

### 7.5 Merge Design Principles

1. **Escalation:** Each higher-order merge is strictly more powerful than its components. A triple merge offers capabilities that no pair merge within it could achieve. A quad is more powerful than any triple within it.

2. **Coherence:** Each merge effect is a natural combination of its constituent forces — not random. If you know what Entropy and Communion do, you should be able to intuit what The Contagion does.

3. **Trade-offs:** Power comes with vulnerability. The strongest merges often have the most dramatic failure modes (The Crusading Empire is overwhelming unless a single link is severed; The Eternal Court is impregnable but cannot adapt).

4. **Playability at Convergence:** The full merge is not a simplified endgame — it's the most complex state, where everything is available and every strategy is viable. The Convergence should feel like the climax of the game, not the epilogue.

5. **Progressive complexity:** Early game (single realms) is the simplest. Players learn individual realm mechanics. Mid game (pair merges) introduces combination effects. Late game (triple+ merges) requires mastery of interactions. The learning curve IS the game's progression.
