# Lore Suggestions

> Each option below presents a different cosmological foundation. For each, I sketch the lore itself and then analyse how it would affect every major design decision: realms, energy systems, player identity, soul mechanics, religion, and endgame.

---

## Option A — The Shattered Will

### The Lore
There was a single, conscious Will that existed before anything else. It was not a god — it was closer to a law of nature that could think. It held all potential within itself: destruction, growth, stillness, hunger, memory, severance. But a conscious Will that contains everything is the same as a Will that wants nothing. So it unmade itself — not out of sacrifice or tragedy, but out of **boredom**. The act of unmaking was the first event, and the fragments of its unmaking became the realms.

Each realm is a shard of one aspect of the original Will. Not an element (fire, water) — an **intent**.

The players are the only witnesses. They were not part of the Will, but they were **adjacent** — they existed in the non-space before the unmaking. The event changed them. They absorbed splinters of intent, becoming semi-conscious echoes of concepts the Will once contained. They are not the Will's children. They are its **residue**.

Souls are what the Will was made of. Every living thing contains a fragment of Will-stuff — a soul. When it dies, that fragment becomes unbound. The players want to absorb this Will-stuff to grow their own splinter, because a large enough splinter might become self-aware — might become a new Will.

### How It Affects Design

| Aspect | Implication |
|--------|------------|
| **Realms (~5-6)** | Each realm embodies an intent: Severance (cutting, separation, individuality), Stagnation (persistence, density, resistance to change), Hunger (consumption, growth, transformation), Fracture (instability, chaos, creativity), Binding (connection, order, control), possibly Absence (void, potential, nothingness) |
| **Energy systems** | Each realm's energy IS its intent. The Hunger realm's energy consumes and transforms things. Severance energy cuts connections. When realms overlap, intents combine — Hunger + Binding might create "controlled consumption" (assimilation), Fracture + Stagnation might create "crystallised chaos" (unpredictable permanence). |
| **Player identity** | Players are asymmetric by nature — each absorbed a different splinter. However, since asymmetry isn't a priority, this can be deferred. Initially all players are generic "residue entities" with the same capabilities. |
| **Souls** | Souls are Will-stuff. Collecting them makes you more real, more powerful. Spending them makes you temporarily weaker but lets you perform acts of will (rites). The tension is perfect: hoard to win, spend to act. |
| **Religion** | Religions are lies that teach inhabitants to direct their Will-stuff (souls) to a specific destination (you) upon death. The belief tags are essentially instructions for how the soul-fragment should behave when freed. |
| **Instant win** | Absorb enough Will-stuff to become a new Will — achieving consciousness, ending the game as a new singular entity. Thematically, this means one player has "won" existence. |
| **Tone** | Philosophical, cosmic, bleak. Not evil — just indifferent. The Will didn't care, and neither does anyone else. Very Souls-like. |

### Realm Sketches (Shattered Will)

1. **The Severance** — A realm of isolated islands, deep chasms, and fractured landscapes. Its inhabitants are individualists — warring city-states, hermit cultures. Energy here CUTS — it can sever connections, break alliances, dissolve religions, isolate nodes. Rites: sever an edge from the graph, disrupt enemy instructions remotely, split a combined node back into two.

2. **The Mire (Stagnation)** — A realm of enduring, ancient civilisations in dense, unchanging landscapes. Everything resists change. Energy here PERSISTS — defences are stronger, decay is slower, but expansion is nearly impossible. Rites: freeze a dominion's state (no decay for several turns), make a node immune to overlap effects, lock down a religion against reform.

3. **The Maw (Hunger)** — A realm of rapid growth and consumption — empires that rise and fall quickly, predatory ecosystems, aggressive expansion. Energy here CONSUMES — it accelerates growth but at a cost. Rites: absorb a neighbouring node into your territory, rapidly spread religion (but it's unstable), consume souls for power boosts.

4. **The Lattice (Binding)** — A realm of complex interconnection — trade networks, alliances, hierarchies, bureaucracies. Energy here CONNECTS — it creates edges, reinforces relationships, builds order. Rites: create a new edge between nodes, establish a spy link, bind two dominions into a cooperative pact.

5. **The Shatter (Fracture)** — A realm of chaos and creativity — unstable terrain, shifting loyalties, revolutionary cultures. Energy here DESTABILISES — unpredictable effects, random mutations, wild magic. Rites: trigger a random event at a target node, scramble enemy instructions, temporarily randomise realm flow near you.

## Option B — The Dreaming Corpse

### The Lore
There was a being — call it the Ur-Entity. It was vast, and it was alive, and then it died. But its death was not an ending. Its body fell apart into realms, each organ or limb becoming a different domain of existence. Its bones became mountains, its blood became oceans, its thoughts became the ambient magic. The realms are its **decomposing body parts**, still faintly twitching with residual life.

The players are **parasites** — organisms that fed on the Ur-Entity when it was alive, and now feed on its corpse. They are not noble. They are not chosen. They are survivors that learned to speak and think by consuming the Ur-Entity's dying thoughts. They are vermin that became gods through opportunistic feeding.

Souls are the Ur-Entity's final heartbeats — each living creature carries one, a fading pulse of the original life-force. When they die, the heartbeat escapes. The players consume these heartbeats to sustain themselves — without them, they would fade into nothing.

### How It Affects Design

| Aspect | Implication |
|--------|------------|
| **Realms (~5-7)** | Each realm is a body part: The Marrow (bones — structure, rigidity), The Bile (digestive system — dissolution, transformation), The Sinew (muscles — movement, force, war), The Cortex (brain — thought, memory, madness), The Ichor (blood — flow, life, connection), The Caul (skin — surface, protection, deception), possibly The Hollow (where the heart was — void, longing) |
| **Energy systems** | Visceral and disgusting. Marrow energy is rigid and structural. Bile energy dissolves and transforms. When realms merge, it's like body parts reattaching — Sinew + Marrow creates "animated skeleton" energy (force + structure), Bile + Cortex creates "dissolving thoughts" (madness, confusion). |
| **Player identity** | Players are parasites — they have no inherent nobility. Their religions are lies. Their dominions are nests. This is VERY Berserk — the God Hand are essentially cosmic parasites feeding on humanity's despair. The aesthetic is gross, fleshy, and Bloodborne-adjacent. |
| **Souls** | Heartbeats — consuming them is literal sustenance. Spending them is risky because you need them to survive, not just win. Could add a minimum soul threshold — below it, you weaken. |
| **Religion** | Religions are **feeding instructions** — they teach inhabitants to worship in ways that direct their heartbeats to the correct parasite upon death. Grotesque when you think about it. |
| **Instant win** | Consume enough heartbeats to reanimate the Ur-Entity with yourself as its consciousness. You don't just win — you BECOME the dead god. |
| **Tone** | Body horror, parasitic, visceral. Very Bloodborne. The world is a corpse and you are a maggot arguing with other maggots over who gets to be the new brain. |

## Option C — The Broken Mirror

### The Lore
Reality was once a single, perfect reflection — a Mirror that showed everything as it truly was. The Mirror didn't create reality; it WAS reality. There was no difference between the reflection and the thing reflected. Then something looked into the Mirror wrong — some impossible observer saw something that contradicted the Mirror, and the contradiction cracked it. Each shard reflects a different distortion of reality — a realm where one aspect is exaggerated, diminished, or inverted.

The players are **the cracks** — the spaces between the shards. They are not beings that live in reality; they are the **gaps in reality** where reality fails to be consistent. They are paradoxes given will. They can move between shards because they exist in the boundaries.

Souls are **reflections** — each living being is a reflection cast by a shard. When they die, the reflection detaches and drifts. The players collect reflections because accumulating enough of them lets a crack expand — lets unreality grow. The player who gathers the most reflections has the widest crack, has the most influence over what reality becomes when the Mirror reassembles.

### How It Affects Design

| Aspect | Implication |
|--------|------------|
| **Realms (~5)** | Each shard is a distortion: The Amplification (everything is MORE — louder, brighter, stronger, more extreme), The Diminishment (everything is less — quieter, smaller, fading), The Inversion (everything is reversed — death heals, growth destroys), The Recursion (everything repeats — loops, cycles, echoes), The Stillness (one frozen moment, stretched infinitely — time doesn't pass, only space exists) |
| **Energy systems** | Each shard's energy IS its distortion — it applies that distortion to actions. Casting a rite in the Amplification makes it bigger but harder to control. In the Diminishment, rites are subtle but precise. In the Inversion, rites have opposite effects. Overlap creates surreal combinations. |
| **Player identity** | Players are abstract and mathematical — they are gaps, contradictions, boundaries. This is the most abstract option. It could feel very unique but also the hardest to make emotionally engaging. |
| **Souls** | Reflections — collecting them is gathering aspects of reality. Spending them is refracting reality to your will. |
| **Religion** | Religions teach inhabitants to believe in a specific distortion — to see reality through a particular crack. When they die, their reflection naturally drifts toward the distortion they believed in. |
| **Instant win** | Accumulate enough reflections to shatter the Mirror entirely — destroying consistent reality and replacing it with your distortion. |
| **Tone** | Surreal, mathematical, abstract. More cerebral than visceral. Less Berserk, more House of Leaves or Borges. May not align well with the Berserk/Souls aesthetic. |

## Option D — The Severed Covenant

### The Lore
There were once many powers — not one that split, but many that were **bound together** by a Covenant. The Covenant was the agreement that held reality coherent: the rules by which energies interacted, the laws by which cause led to effect, the grammar of existence. Under the Covenant, the powers coexisted in a single, harmonious realm. But one of the powers violated the Covenant — deliberately, knowingly, for reasons now lost — and the violation shattered the agreement. Without the Covenant, the powers could no longer coexist. Reality fragmented into separate realms, each built around one power's nature, no longer constrained by the rules of coexistence.

The players were the **arbiters** of the Covenant — entities tasked with enforcing the agreement. When the Covenant shattered, they lost their purpose and their authority. Now they wander the fragments, trying to rebuild influence. They are not above the powers — they are **former servants of a dead law**, scrambling for relevance in a lawless multiverse. They use dominions and religions as tools to impose fragments of order — not because they believe in order, but because order gives them power.

Souls are the **signatures** on the Covenant — every living being carries a signature, an implicit agreement to the rules of reality. When they die, the signature is released. The players collect signatures because enough of them could be used to **rewrite a new Covenant** — one that places them at the top.

### How It Affects Design

| Aspect | Implication |
|--------|------------|
| **Realms (~5-6)** | Each realm is organised around a power: Dominion (authority, hierarchy, control), Entropy (decay, change, renewal), Communion (connection, empathy, absorption), Wrath (conflict, passion, destruction), Memory (preservation, knowledge, stasis), possibly Void (the absence of any power — the realm that formed where the Covenant itself lived) |
| **Energy systems** | Each power defines the rules of its realm. Dominion energy makes hierarchies strong. Entropy energy makes everything temporary. Overlap creates contested rule-sets — Dominion + Entropy means "authority that decays" (temporary alliances, leadership that must be constantly reinforced). The rule-mutation mechanic is baked directly into the lore. |
| **Player identity** | Former law enforcers gone rogue. They understand rules and systems better than anyone — which is why they're so good at exploiting them. This gives a natural reason for the conditional instruction system: they are literally beings who think in rules and conditions. It also explains why they can manipulate religions — they understand belief systems as legal frameworks. |
| **Souls** | Covenant signatures — collecting them is accumulating legal authority. Spending them is invoking clauses of a dead law. Fits naturally with rites (rites are old Covenant powers, fuelled by the authority of collected signatures). |
| **Religion** | Religions are **codes of law** — they teach inhabitants to believe in a set of rules. When inhabitants die believing in those rules, their signature (soul) goes to the arbiter who wrote those rules. This makes doctrine creation feel like legislation, which fits the Covenant theme. |
| **Instant win** | Collect enough signatures to write a new Covenant — forcing all realms to reunify under YOUR rules. You don't just win; you become the new law of reality. |
| **Tone** | Legal-cosmic horror. The universe is a broken contract and you're a disbarred lawyer trying to become judge, jury, and executioner. Darker and weirder than standard fantasy. Very compatible with the Souls aesthetic of ruin, former glory, and corrupted purpose. |

## Comparison Matrix

| | Shattered Will | Dreaming Corpse | Broken Mirror | Severed Covenant |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Tone** | Philosophical, cosmic, indifferent | Body horror, parasitic, visceral | Surreal, mathematical, abstract | Legal-cosmic horror, ruined authority |
| **Berserk/Souls fit** | ★★★★☆ | ★★★★★ | ★★☆☆☆ | ★★★★★ |
| **Realm identity** | Intents (abstract) | Body parts (visceral) | Distortions (surreal) | Powers (thematic) |
| **Energy system clarity** | High — intents are intuitive | High — body metaphors are vivid | Medium — distortions are harder to parse | Very high — powers define rules directly |
| **Player motivation** | Become real | Survive / become the corpse | Expand your crack | Rewrite the law |
| **Soul explanation** | Will-stuff (clean) | Heartbeats (visceral) | Reflections (abstract) | Signatures (legal) |
| **Rule mutation fit** | Medium — intents don't inherently change rules | Medium — body parts don't obviously change game rules | High — distortions are literally rule changes | Very high — the whole lore is about rules |
| **Religion feel** | Philosophical manipulation | Feeding strategy | Reality distortion | Legal code / doctrine |
| **Instant win clarity** | Become a new Will | Reanimate the corpse | Shatter reality | Rewrite the Covenant |
| **Emotional resonance** | Medium-high | Very high | Low-medium | High |
| **Uniqueness** | Medium | High | Very high | High |

> [!TIP]
> **Options A (Shattered Will) and D (Severed Covenant) are the strongest fits** for the game as designed. Option A gives the cleanest soul/power framework with intuitive realm identities. Option D has the strongest mechanical alignment — the whole lore IS about rules, conditions, and broken systems, which mirrors the game's instruction-based mechanics perfectly. Option B is the most visceral and Bloodborne-adjacent but might be tonally too narrow. Option C is the most unique but hardest to make feel like a board game.

## Hybrid Possibility

Options A and D could be combined: The original Will created the Covenant as the structure of its own consciousness. When the Will died / unmade itself, the Covenant shattered, and the fragments of both (intents AND rules) scattered into realms. Players are former arbiters of the Will's internal consistency — now loose in a broken system, armed with the ability to understand and manipulate conditions and rules, feeding on the fragments of consciousness (souls) that the system left behind.

This gives you:
- The **philosophical weight** of Option A (a dead Will, cosmic indifference)
- The **mechanical alignment** of Option D (rules, covenants, conditions)
- A natural explanation for **every game system** (instructions = covenant enforcement, religions = competing rule-sets, soul spending = invoking fragments of the old law, realm overlap = rules colliding)
